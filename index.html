About the Author

The person
behind the page

Andre Venture is an Australian author and illustrator who loves taking complex ideas apart and putting them back together on the page. His background in IT and corporate education taught him how to untangle complicated systems and explain them to anyone — a skill he now applies to building rich, intricate fictional worlds.

When he isn't writing gripping fiction for adults, Andre writes and illustrates whimsical children's poetry about astronomy and the solar system, driven by the firm belief that the cosmos is simply too fascinating to leave to the grown-ups.

Rara Raccoon
Exploring the Moon

Rara Raccoon Series — Book 1

"Is the Moon actually made of cheese?"

Rara Raccoon has a big question and a shiny green rocket, and she is headed straight for the stars to find the answer! Join Rara on her very first mission to the Moon, where she discovers that space is not just dark and quiet — it is full of bouncy gravity, crunchy moon dust, and giant moon bowls.

Written in a playful, rhyming style, this book invites little explorers to follow Rara's poetic journey across the lunar surface. Your child will learn real science facts through engaging verse as Rara investigates regolith, explores craters, and discovers why the Moon is so quiet.

STEM Made Simple: Complex concepts like vacuums and gravity are explained through wonder and curiosity.

Rhyme and Rhythm: The poetic format makes it a perfect read-aloud book for bedtime.

Stunning Artwork: Beautifully illustrated with original designs and AI assistance for a truly boutique feel.

Legacy Hardware

Standalone Sci-Fi Novel

Sam wasn't a hero. He was the IT guy.

Then he tripped in a research lab, fell into an experimental resin, and spent seven hundred years sealed inside a crystal — conscious for every minute of it.

The civilization that finally cracks him open is luminous, elegant, and quietly dying. Across the galaxy, colonies are going silent. Not destroyed — erased, as if they had never existed at all. And the fragile people who inherited the future can't survive the journey to find out why.

Sam can. He's spent his whole career being handed broken things nobody else could diagnose. This is just a bigger ticket.

So he takes it: one stubborn twenty-first-century body, one borrowed starship he isn't licensed to fly, and one jailbroken museum robot with a gift for deadpan and an inconvenient habit of loyalty.

The universe was never Sam's to fix. How far will he go to fix it anyway?

For readers who love the competent, funny, big-hearted science fiction of Andy Weir and Dennis E. Taylor.
